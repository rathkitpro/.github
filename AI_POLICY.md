# AI policy

How the rathkitpro organization expects contributors to use AI tools such as coding agents and chat assistants. It
applies to every contributor and maintainer in every Rathkit repository, and it is part of the
contribution rules in [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md).

## AI use is assumed

We use AI tools and expect you may too. You do not have to disclose that you used one, and we
will not ask. What matters is the quality of the change and the quality of the conversation about
it.

## No AI trailers or footers

A commit carries one trailer: your own `Signed-off-by` line. Do not add `Co-authored-by` trailers
for tools, model names, session links or "Generated with" footers to commits, pull request
descriptions or comments.

The sign-off is a statement by a person that they have the right to submit the work (see
[DCO](DCO)). A tool cannot make that statement, and the history should record who is responsible.

## You own it

- **Understand every line you submit.** If you cannot explain what a change does and why without
  asking the assistant, it is not ready.
- **Test it yourself.** Review and run your whole change before you open the pull request and
  again after every update. A maintainer should not be the first person to read it closely.
- **Use the templates.** A pull request or issue with the template deleted or left empty tells us
  nobody looked at it.
- **"My agent wrote it" is not an answer** to a review comment.

## Talk to us yourself

- **Write your own issues, descriptions and replies.** Using a tool to fix your grammar or
  translate is fine. The thinking has to be yours, and it should read like it.
- **Do not paste AI-generated replies** to maintainers or other contributors, and do not post an
  AI-generated review of someone else's pull request. Using a tool to help you understand a
  change is fine. Post only a review you agree with and can defend.
- **Keep it short.** Say what the problem is, what you tried and what you are unsure about. Long
  generated text hides the point.
- **Comments in code explain why,** not how you got there. Keep the story of your process in the
  pull request description.

## Respect maintainer time

- **Discuss first.** Beyond a small fix, get an issue accepted before you write the change (see
  [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md)).
- **Keep changes small and focused.**
- **Do not flood us.** If you have pull requests open that are not merged, do not open more. This
  matters most for new contributors.

## Your right to submit

Tools can reproduce code from other projects without telling you. The DCO says you have the right
to submit your change, so you are responsible for that. Do not submit anything you cannot trace
back to yourself, and do not submit code that is copied or closely derived from another project
unless its license allows it and you say so in the pull request.

## Enforcement

We may close issues and pull requests that do not follow this policy, without a detailed
explanation, and we will not debate it. Contributors who keep ignoring it will be blocked.

This policy is not aimed at newcomers who are learning and ask honest questions. We want to help
those people.
